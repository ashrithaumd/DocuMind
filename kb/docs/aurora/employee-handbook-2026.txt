Aurora Robotics — Employee Handbook

Version 2026.1 — for the year 2026. Effective 1 March 2026.

Owner: the People team. Applies to every employee of Aurora Robotics.

1. About this handbook

This handbook describes how we work at Aurora Robotics: the rules everyone follows, the benefits
you can count on, and where to go when something is unclear. It is version 2026.1 of the
handbook, written for the year 2026 and effective from 1 March 2026. It replaces every
earlier version. Where this handbook and an older team page disagree, this handbook wins;
where it and the law of the place you work disagree, the law wins and the People team will
tell you how.

We review the handbook once a year. Suggestions are welcome at any time: open a request in
PeopleDesk or write to the People team. Small corrections are published straight away and
listed in the change log at the end; anything that changes a benefit or an entitlement waits
for the next yearly version, so nobody loses something they planned around in the middle of
the year.

A note on tone. We tried to write this the way we would explain it to a new colleague over
coffee, not the way a lawyer would. When a sentence matters legally, it says so plainly.

2. Our working hours

Most of us work about forty hours a week. We do not track hours for salaried staff, and we
do not reward people for being online late. What we ask is that you are reachable during your
team's core hours, that you keep your calendar honest, and that you tell your team when you
will be away.

Teams agree their own core hours and publish them on their team page. Meetings outside core
hours need a good reason and should rotate, so the same people are not always the ones on
an early or late call.

Under this version of the handbook, people whose role allows it may work away from the office
for up to 3 days a week. Field and lab roles that need hands on hardware agree a
pattern with their manager instead. Working from another country is covered under "Travel
and working abroad" below.

3. Paid time off

Every full-time employee receives 22 days of paid vacation each calendar year, on top
of public holidays. Part-time colleagues receive the same number pro rata. Vacation builds up
evenly through the year, but you may take days before you have built them up, as long as your
manager agrees.

You may carry up to 5 unused vacation days into the next year. Carried-over days
must be used by 31 March or they lapse; we would rather you rested than banked days.

Please give at least two weeks' notice for any absence of more than three days, and record
every day off in PeopleDesk so payroll and your team both know. Managers should answer a
request within three working days. A request that has not been answered in that time may be
escalated to the People team.

4. When you are unwell

If you are ill, stay home and get better. You have 10 paid sick days a year, and you do
not need a doctor's note for absences of up to three consecutive days. For longer absences,
the People team will ask for a note and will check in with you about what support you need.

Tell your manager as early as you can on the first day, by message or phone, and log the day
in PeopleDesk when you are able to. Sick days are separate from vacation: if you fall ill while
on vacation, tell us and the days you were ill are given back to you as vacation.

For longer illnesses, our disability cover through Keystone Health pays 60 percent of your
salary after the sick days run out, for up to 26 weeks.

5. Family leave

A parent who has a new child, by birth, adoption or surrogacy, can take 16 weeks of
leave on full pay, at any time in the first year. It can be taken in one block or in up to
three separate blocks. Both parents receive the same entitlement, whatever their gender.

Colleagues who become the main carer for a close relative with a serious, long-lasting illness
can step away from work on full pay for up to 10 weeks in any twelve-month period.
The weeks do not have to be taken together, and your role is kept open for you.

When someone close to you dies, take the time you need. We give 5 paid days of
bereavement leave as a minimum, and your manager can agree more.

6. Pay and payroll

Salaries are paid monthly, on the last working day of the month, into the bank account you
registered in PeopleDesk. Your payslip appears in PeopleDesk two days before payday. If a
payment is wrong, tell the People team straight away; underpayments are corrected within five
working days, outside the normal payroll run if needed.

Salary bands are reviewed once a year. Changes from the yearly review take effect on the
first payroll after the review closes. Your manager will explain where your pay sits in your
band and what would move it.

7. Retirement savings

We match what you put into the company retirement plan, up to 5 percent of your base
salary. Matching contributions belong to you immediately from the day they are paid in. You can change how much
you contribute at any time in PeopleDesk; changes apply from the next payroll.

The plan is run by an outside administrator. the People team holds two information sessions a
year and can arrange a free session with an independent adviser.

8. Health and wellbeing

Health, dental and vision cover is provided through Keystone Health. We pay the full premium
for employees and 80 percent for partners and children. Cover starts on your first day.

Everyone also receives a wellbeing allowance of 60 US dollars a month, which can be
spent on gym membership, sports classes, therapy, meditation apps or similar. Claim it through
PeopleDesk with a receipt; unused allowance does not roll over.

Our employee assistance programme offers free, confidential counselling, around the clock,
for you and your household. Nobody at Aurora sees who uses it.

9. Learning and growth

Each employee has a learning budget of 2000 US dollars a year for courses, books,
conferences and certifications related to their work or the next role they are aiming for.
Spending up to the budget only needs your manager's agreement; anything above it needs a
short written case.

Performance conversations happen twice a year, in April and October. They are written down, shared with
you, and never a surprise: anything serious should already have come up in your regular
one-to-ones. Promotions are decided in the same cycles.

After 5 years with us, you can take a paid sabbatical of 4
weeks, once in every 5-year period, to rest, travel or learn something new.

10. Travel and expenses

Book travel through the company portal so we can find you if something goes wrong on a trip.
The full rules are in the expense policy; the headline numbers for 2026 are a meal allowance
of 75 US dollars a day, a hotel cap of 220 US dollars a night (310 in the most
expensive cities), and mileage at 0.62 US dollars a mile for your own car.

Claims must be submitted within 30 days of the expense, with a receipt. We reimburse
you in the next payroll. A company card is available for people who travel more than four
times a year; ask the People team.

Every employee also gets 50 US dollars a month towards their mobile phone bill, paid
automatically with salary.

11. Travel and working abroad

Short trips abroad for work are booked like any other travel. Working from another country
while not on a work trip is different: it can create tax and immigration obligations for you
and for us. You may work from another country for up to 30 days in a calendar year without
special approval, as long as you tell the People team before you go. Anything longer needs
approval from the People team and our legal adviser, in writing, before you travel.

12. Equipment and security

You will receive a laptop and the accessories you need on or before your first day. It
belongs to Aurora and must be returned when you leave. Keep it locked when you step away, and
never let anyone else use it, including family members.

Report a lost or stolen device to it@aurora-robotics.example as soon as you notice, whatever the time. We would
much rather hear about a lost laptop in the first hour than the first week: we can wipe it
remotely and nobody will be blamed for reporting quickly.

The information security policy has the full rules, including passwords, multi-factor
authentication and how to handle customer data.

13. Behaviour and respect

We expect everyone to treat colleagues, customers and partners with respect. Harassment,
discrimination and bullying are not tolerated, whoever is responsible and whatever their
role. If something happens to you or you see it happen, you can raise it with your manager,
with the People team, or anonymously through the ethics line run by an outside provider.

Every concern is looked into, quickly and fairly. Nobody who raises a concern in good faith
will suffer for it; retaliation is itself a serious breach of this handbook.

14. Referrals

The best people we hire are often people you already know. If someone you referred is hired
and stays for six months, you receive a referral bonus of 3000 US dollars, paid with the
next salary. Referrals for leadership roles are welcome but do not earn the bonus.

15. Volunteering and public duties

Each year you can take 2 paid days to volunteer for a charity or community
group of your choice. If you are called for jury duty, we pay your full salary for up to
10 working days, and longer if the law where you work requires it.

16. Public holidays

Offices follow the public holidays of the country they are in, and the list for 2026 is
published in Notion each December. If you work remotely, you follow the holidays of the
country you live and are employed in. If your role means you must work on a public holiday,
you receive a day off in lieu, to be taken within three months.

17. Gifts and hospitality

You may accept small gifts from customers or suppliers worth up to 75 US dollars, as
long as they are not cash and nothing is expected in return. Anything worth more must be
declared in PeopleDesk within five working days, and usually politely returned. Meals with a
supplier are fine if they are occasional and the conversation is about work; never accept
hospitality from a supplier while a purchasing decision involving them is open.

18. Conflicts of interest

Tell the People team about anything that could make it look as if your personal interests and
your work pull in different directions: a second job, a stake in a supplier or competitor, or
a close relative working for one. A declared conflict is rarely a problem; an undeclared one
always is. Declarations are reviewed by the People team and your manager within ten working days.

19. Side projects and open source

What you build in your own time, on your own equipment, without using Aurora's confidential
information, belongs to you. Contributing to open-source projects is encouraged; for projects
related to our products, check with your manager first so we do not accidentally publish
something we have filed for patent protection.

20. Social media and speaking

You are welcome to say where you work and to share public news about Aurora. Do not share
anything about customers, unreleased products or internal numbers. Only the communications
team speaks for the company to journalists. If you are asked to speak at a conference about
your work, tell your manager; we will usually pay the travel from your learning budget.

21. Personal relationships at work

Relationships between colleagues are a private matter, except where one person has authority
over the other's pay, promotion or work. In that case, the more senior person must tell
the People team, in confidence, so the reporting line can be changed. Nobody will be penalised
for disclosing a relationship.

22. Health and safety

Anyone who enters the robot test floor must have completed the floor safety induction, wear safety shoes, and stay outside the yellow lines while robots are moving. Never step into the path of a moving robot to stop it: use the red emergency-stop buttons on every pillar.
Report every accident and every near miss within 24 hours using the safety form, even if
nobody was hurt. Near-miss reports are how we stop the next accident; they are never used to
blame anyone. First-aid kits and defibrillators are marked on the floor plans in Notion.

23. Data handling and privacy

Customer data is the most sensitive thing we hold. Only access the customer data you need for
the task in front of you, never copy it to personal devices or personal accounts, and never
paste it into tools that are not on the approved list in Notion. Employee data is handled by
the People team only. If you think data has been exposed, report it within the hour, even if you
are not sure; we would rather investigate a false alarm.

24. Performance and promotion

Each review cycle has three steps: a short self-assessment, feedback from two or three
colleagues you choose with your manager, and a conversation with your manager. Ratings are on
a four-point scale: "exceeds", "strong", "developing" and "not meeting". A "not meeting" rating
always comes with a written improvement plan of at least eight weeks and regular check-ins.

Promotions are proposed by managers and reviewed by a panel of senior colleagues from other
teams, so that standards are the same across the company. A promotion raises your salary by at
least 8 percent, or to the bottom of the new band if that is higher.

25. Compensation reviews

Salaries are reviewed once a year against the market and against the bands for each level.
Our aim is to pay at or above the median of the market for every role. Merit increases are
decided from the yearly budget, and are higher for people low in their band who are performing
strongly. Equity is reviewed at the same time.

26. Parking, commuting and the office

The Pittsburgh office has 120 parking spaces, free for employees, and a secure bike room. Public transport passes are reimbursed up to 100 US dollars a month.
The office kitchen is stocked every weekday; please clean up after yourself. Quiet rooms can be
booked for up to two hours at a time. Pets are not allowed in the office, except assistance
animals.

27. Leaving Aurora

If you decide to leave, please give at least 2 weeks' notice in writing to your
manager, or the notice period in your contract if that is longer. We will pay out vacation
days you have built up and not used. Return your equipment on or before your last day; the
IT team will send a prepaid box if you work remotely.

We hold a short exit conversation with everyone who leaves. It is optional, but it is one of
the most useful sources of honest feedback we have.

28. Frequently asked questions

Q: Can I take vacation in my first month?
A: Yes. Vacation builds up through the year, but you can take days early with your manager's agreement. If you leave before you have built them up, the difference is deducted from your final pay.

Q: Do public holidays count against my vacation days?
A: No. Public holidays are on top of your 22 vacation days.

Q: My child is ill. Is that a sick day?
A: Short absences to look after an ill child can be logged as sick days. For a long-term illness of a close relative, see family leave.

Q: Who approves a training course?
A: Your manager, as long as it fits in your 2000 US dollar budget for the year.

Q: How fast are expenses reimbursed?
A: In the next payroll after your claim is approved. Submit within 30 days.

Q: Can I work from a café or a coworking space?
A: Yes, as long as you can take calls privately and you never leave your laptop unattended.

Q: What happens to my retirement match if I leave?
A: Matching contributions are yours from the day they are paid in, and stay in the plan or can be moved.

Q: Can I buy extra vacation days?
A: Not under this version of the handbook. Unpaid leave of up to four weeks can be agreed with your manager.

Q: Who do I tell if I am going to be late or absent?
A: Your manager, as early as you can, and your team channel in Slack.

Q: A supplier sent me a bottle of wine. Can I keep it?
A: If it is worth no more than 75 US dollars and nothing is expected in return, yes. Otherwise declare it in PeopleDesk within five working days.

Q: Can I work a second job?
A: Usually yes, as long as it doesn't compete with us or affect your work. Declare it as a potential conflict of interest first.

Q: How do I get a second monitor or a better chair?
A: Ask it@aurora-robotics.example. Ergonomic equipment recommended by an assessment is always approved.

Q: When does a promotion take effect?
A: From the first payroll after the review cycle in which it was decided, with a raise of at least 8 percent.

Q: I lost my laptop on the train. What now?
A: Tell it@aurora-robotics.example straight away, whatever the time. Nobody is blamed for reporting quickly.

Q: Can I bring my dog to the office?
A: Not under this version of the handbook, except assistance animals.

Q: Is the wellbeing allowance taxable?
A: In most countries it is paid as a taxable benefit through payroll. the People team can explain the rules where you live.

Q: How long do I have to submit a receipt?
A: 30 days from the date of the expense.

Q: Who can see my employee assistance programme sessions?
A: Nobody at Aurora. The provider only reports anonymous totals.

Q: Where are the latest versions of company policies?
A: On the policies page of Notion. This handbook is version 2026.1.
