Pika picker, generation 2 — service manual

Version 2.9, 2024. Published by Aurora Robotics. This edition replaces all earlier versions of the Pika gen 2 service manual.

1. How to use this document

This document is for the people who keep Pika gen 2 running: field technicians and trained site staff. Section 2 lists the
key specifications. Section 3 covers safety and access. Section 4 is the reference of
every error code, with what it means, the usual causes and what to do. Section 5 lists
the replaceable parts with their identifiers and service intervals. Section 6 has step-by-step
procedures, and section 7 the maintenance schedule. When you open a ticket about
Pika gen 2, always quote the error code and the identifier from these tables exactly as
written: our tooling searches on them.

2. Key specifications

- Maximum payload: 8 kg
- Pick rate: up to 450 items per hour
- Shelf reach: 0.3 m to 2.1 m
- Battery: 5 hours picking, 70 minutes to 80 percent
- Weight: 139 kg
- Top speed: 1.5 m/s
- Ingress rating: IP42
- Operating temperature: 5 to 35 degrees Celsius

3. Safety and access

Lock out the unit with the physical key before opening any panel. Two people are needed for any work above 1.5 m or any lift of more than 25 kg. Wear safety shoes on the robot floor at all times.
Every intervention is recorded against a ticket in the FOPS queue. A ticket id looks like
FOPS-86155. Never close a ticket without recording what was changed, and never
change two things at once: if the problem comes back, you need to know which change mattered.

4. Error code reference

4.1 Drive errors

E-1900 WHEEL_SLIP. Raised by the drive subsystem. Usual cause: a worn component past its service interval. First check: confirm the unit is on the current release. Severity: warning. Needs a manual reset from the service menu.

E-1901 MOTOR_OVERCURRENT. Raised by the drive subsystem. Usual cause: intermittent network loss. First check: confirm the unit is on the current release. Severity: warning. Logged only; no action unless it repeats more than 3 times an hour.

E-1202 ENCODER_MISMATCH. Raised by the drive subsystem. Usual cause: a worn component past its service interval. First check: compare the configuration with the last known-good snapshot. Severity: critical. Stops the unit until acknowledged.

4.7 Troubleshooting by symptom

Symptom (drive): The robot moves in a slightly curved line when it should go straight.
What to do: Look at the drive first: uneven wear or a dirty sensor is the usual reason. Recalibrate after cleaning.

Symptom (drive): It takes much longer than usual to be ready after charging.
What to do: Check the drive temperature readings; a pack that is too cold or too hot charges slowly by design.

Symptom (drive): The robot keeps letting go of soft plastic bags just after lifting them.
What to do: Check the drive for leaks around the cups and for a pump that sounds weaker than usual; see the error reference for the matching code.

Symptom (drive): The status light blinks amber but the tablet shows no error.
What to do: Open the service menu and read the drive warnings, which do not always raise a visible error.

Symptom (gripper): Staff report a grinding or clicking sound during moves.

6. Procedures

6.1 Replacing the battery pack
  1. Drive Pika gen 2 to the service bay and stop it with the service tablet.
  2. Turn the key to LOCK and wait for the status light to turn solid blue.
  3. Release the two pack latches on the left side.
  4. Slide the pack out on the rails; it weighs about 24 kg, so use the lift trolley.
  5. Slide the new pack in until both latches click.
  6. Turn the key to RUN and check the pack serial number in Skyview.
  7. Return the old pack to the take-back crate and log its serial on the ticket.

6.2 Calibrating the depth cameras
  1. Place the checkerboard target 1.0 m in front of the robot, centred.
  2. Open Service > Vision > Calibrate on the tablet.
  3. Hold the target still while the progress bar fills, about 90 seconds.
  4. Move the target to 1.5 m and repeat.
  5. Accept the result only if the reprojection error shown is under 0.4 pixels.

6.3 Recovering from a lost localization
  1. Stop the robot and check that the lidar window is clean.
  2. Drive it by hand to the nearest charging dock, which is a known position.
  3. Choose Service > Navigation > Relocalize.
  4. If relocalization fails twice, re-run floor-map alignment and raise a ticket.

6.4 Updating firmware on site
  1. Check the release notes for the target version and any special steps.
  2. Dock the robot and keep it charging during the update.
  3. Start the update from Skyview, never from a USB stick.
  4. Do not interrupt power for the 12 minutes the update takes.
  5. Run the self-test and send one test mission before returning the robot to service.

6.5 Investigating repeated drive errors
  1. List every drive error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Run the built-in self-test from the service menu.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a site change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

6.6 Investigating repeated gripper errors
  1. List every gripper error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Clean the sensor surface with a dry microfibre cloth.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a network change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

6.7 Investigating repeated vision errors
  1. List every vision error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Compare the configuration with the last known-good snapshot.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a schema change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

6.8 Investigating repeated battery errors
  1. List every battery error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Check the event log for the five minutes before the error.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a site change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

6.9 Investigating repeated lift mast errors
  1. List every lift mast error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Confirm the unit is on the current release.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a configuration change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

6.10 Investigating repeated network errors
  1. List every network error from the last 7 days and group them by code.
  2. Check the site network dashboard for drops.
  3. Check whether the errors started after a release or a network change.
  4. Fix one cause at a time and watch for 24 hours before closing the ticket.

7. Maintenance schedule

Daily: check the vision; inspect the drive; review the network event log for repeated warnings.

Weekly: check the lift mast; inspect the vision; review the battery event log for repeated warnings.

Every 500 operating hours: check the drive; inspect the vision; review the gripper event log for repeated warnings.

Every 2,000 operating hours: check the network; inspect the drive; review the battery event log for repeated warnings.

Yearly: check the lift mast; inspect the network; review the gripper event log for repeated warnings.
